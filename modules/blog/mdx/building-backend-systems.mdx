Good backend systems are usually calm. They make the common path obvious, keep failure modes visible, and avoid cleverness where a plain interface would do.

When I build services, I try to keep a few things consistent:

- clear boundaries between transport, business logic, and persistence
- logs and metrics that explain what the system is doing
- small APIs that are easy to test and reason about
- operational defaults that help during incidents

That kind of simplicity is not basic. It is the work that makes a system easier to trust when traffic grows, requirements change, or something breaks at the worst possible time.
