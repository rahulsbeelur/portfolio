Kafka consumer lag is easy to collect and surprisingly easy to misunderstand. A large number might mean a consumer is unhealthy, or it might be a short burst that the system is already processing.

The first useful question is not “How large is the lag?” It is “What is the lag doing?”

I want a queue-health view to answer four things quickly:

-   Is lag growing, flat, or falling?
-   How long has that direction continued?
-   Which consumer and partitions are responsible?
-   Is throughput high enough to recover?

## Direction before thresholds

A static threshold treats every workload the same. A timeline gives the number context. Growing lag with falling consumer throughput is very different from a brief spike followed by a steady recovery.

Alerting should combine the amount of lag with its duration and direction. That avoids waking someone for a queue that is already healing while still catching a smaller backlog that has quietly grown for an hour.

## Explain the next action

The best dashboard does not stop at “lag: 42,000.” It says which consumer changed, when the change began, and whether a deploy or traffic spike lines up with it.

Metrics become useful when they shorten the path from noticing a problem to deciding what to do next.
