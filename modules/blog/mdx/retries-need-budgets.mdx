Retries are useful when a failure is temporary. They are dangerous when every caller retries immediately, indefinitely, and at the same time.

A good retry policy answers three questions:

1. Which failures are actually safe to retry?
2. How much additional work may this request create?
3. When should the system stop and surface the failure?

Exponential backoff spreads attempts over time. Jitter prevents many clients from lining up on the same schedule. A retry budget limits amplification by putting a ceiling on the extra traffic created during failure.

## Respect the original deadline

A request with two seconds remaining should not start a retry that may take five seconds. Each attempt needs to fit within the caller’s overall deadline, including connection time and expected processing time.

Retries also need idempotent operations. Repeating a read is usually safe; repeating a state-changing action may require an idempotency key or a different recovery path.

The goal is graceful recovery, not persistence at any cost. Sometimes the reliable choice is to stop, return a clear error, and let a higher-level workflow decide what happens next.
